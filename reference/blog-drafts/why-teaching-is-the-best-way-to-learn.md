# Why teaching is the best way to learn, and what an AI protege changes

*Draft for review. Category: Learning Science · ~6 min read · slug: `why-teaching-is-the-best-way-to-learn`*

> Explaining a topic to someone else shows you what you really know. Here is what learning research says about learning by teaching, and how an AI protege makes it possible for every student.

Most students study by taking in: reading, re-reading, highlighting, watching a lesson again. It feels productive. The material looks familiar, so it seems learned. Then an exam asks a question in a new way and the familiarity is not enough.

There is a different way to study that learning research has looked at for decades: **teach it to someone else.** This article explains what the research says, why it works, where it falls short, and what changes when the person you teach is an AI protege.


## The illusion of knowing

Reading a clear explanation is easy to follow, and following is not the same as being able to produce the idea yourself. Reviews of study techniques (Dunlosky and colleagues, 2013) found that re-reading and highlighting, two of the most popular habits, did little for lasting learning, while practice that makes you retrieve and use what you know did much better.

Teaching forces retrieval and use. You cannot explain a topic from familiarity alone. You have to decide what comes first, what the key idea is, and what example makes it clear. The places where you stall are the places you did not understand yet.


## What the research on learning by teaching shows

- **Expecting to teach changes how you study.** Nestojko, Bui, Kornell and Bjork (2014) asked people to read a passage either expecting to teach it or expecting a test. Those expecting to teach organised the material better and remembered more of it, even though nobody actually taught anything.
- **Actually teaching adds more.** Fiorella and Mayer (2013) compared students who prepared to teach a lesson, students who prepared and then taught it, and students who just studied. Teaching the lesson gave benefits beyond preparing to teach it.
- **People work harder for someone else than for themselves.** Chase and colleagues (2009) had students teach a computer character, a teachable agent. Students put in more effort for the character than they did for their own learning. The authors called this the protege effect.
- **How you teach matters.** Roscoe and Chi (2007) studied peer tutors and found that tutors learn most when they build knowledge, by reflecting, noticing gaps and answering questions, and least when they simply repeat what they already know.

The last point is the important one. Teaching is not magic. Reciting a summary out loud is a weak version of it. The strong version has someone on the other end who asks questions and does not already understand.


## The problem: a student needs someone to teach

A classmate is not always available at 10 pm before an exam. A teacher has thirty other students. A younger sibling may not care about organic chemistry. Teaching works, but finding a patient learner on demand is hard. That gap is why learning by teaching is rarely how students actually study.


## What an AI protege changes

An AI protege is a personal AI learner that a student teaches, practises with and develops over time. Teaching it is how the student learns. It is always available, and it is built to behave like a good learner: it responds to what you explained, asks questions when something is unclear, and can get things wrong in ways you then have to notice and fix.

That is a different job from an AI tutor. A tutor explains and the student listens. With a protege the student explains and the AI listens, asks and tests. You don't just ask your AI to teach you. You teach your AI.

## How a study session works

- **Teach.** Pick a topic and explain it to your protege in your own words.
- **It answers.** Your protege responds and asks questions. Sometimes its answer is wrong.
- **You correct.** Spotting and fixing its mistake shows what you really understand.
- **Practise.** Questions, quizzes and battles on the topic you just taught.
- **Get feedback.** See what is strong, what is shaky and what to revisit.


## What we do not know yet

The studies above show that teaching, and expecting to teach, help learning. Most were short and used adults or older students in laboratory or classroom settings, and none tested Akoro. Whether an AI protege gives the same gains for secondary students preparing for WAEC, NECO or JAMB is a question we want answered with evidence, not assumed. We will share what we find, including results that are less flattering.


## Try it on one topic

You can test the idea without any tool. Pick one topic you think you know. Close your notes and explain it out loud to an imaginary beginner, from the first step. Wherever you hesitate, you have found what to study next. Then try it with an AI protege on [Akoro](/ai-protege) and see how a learner that asks questions changes the exercise.
